# Ideas

Moves to borrow from when pitching directions. None of them is required, and no reel should use all of them. Mix, bend or ignore them; invent new ones. Two reels in a row should not look alike.

Each move is a principle, written without any page's look. Borrow the principle, never a page's signature move (its character, palette, layout or catchphrase): a copy reads as a copy, and the page we copy from is always the better version of itself.

## The opening

- **Start in motion.** The first words are already writing on in frame 1, or the ground, the moon or the key object climbs into frame. Never open on a still title card.
- **The situation is on screen at 0.0 s.** The premise line is readable in the first frame; one swap at about 1.3 s turns it ("Everyone's getting married." then "Except you.").
- **Show the empty box first.** An empty slot (a blank count, an empty circle, a missing link) asks the question before any words do. A hub marked "MISSING" with the question around it, and cards that fill in the cost of the gap, is the same move made bigger.
- **Show the answer's shape in the hook.** A small ordered tracker under the question ("1 · chest, 2 · tongue"), reused later as chapter labels.
- **Where and when, in two sizes.** A big place name, then a small time line under it, in the first 2 s. Strong for seerah.
- **Name the problem as chips.** Three one-word chips pop in round a lit centre, then one line clears them.
- **A huge number beside a zero.** Contrast reads in one second.
- **Caught mid-action.** At 0.0 s the object is already falling, pouring or opening, so the frame reads as a story already happening.
- **One bright accent on a calm field.** A single lit point or colour where the eye lands first.
- **Push through a surface.** The camera moves through a door, a page or water into the scene in the first second.
- **A counter already running.** A number ticks before the first word arrives.
- **Text that settles.** A word arrives rough (a wobble, a half-drawn stroke) and snaps clean, a built-in swap.
- **Sound at 0.0 s.** One soft hit, whoosh or the voice starting on frame 1, never a slow fade-in; inside the sound limits (rules.md, Sound).
- **The bed drops out on the key word,** then returns. Silence where sound was expected makes the viewer look.
- **Avoid the "we scanned the Qur'an" ring, keep the format.** The ring (a radial bar per surah, length = ayah count, a counter running through the surahs, hits lighting up as labels) is on many pages, so it reads as a copy. The format itself is open: see "The theme scan" in story-shapes.md.
- **Our own count: lanterns in the hall.** A count is a set of lanterns that light, never a ring or bars. To count through the Qur'an, the camera flies down a hall while lanterns light ahead of it, left and right in turn, with a counter; a miss stays a dark lantern with a small label ("9 · AT-TAWBAH"). To compare two counts, hang strings of small lanterns, the same number on each string, so the bigger count is more strings ("57" = 3 strings of 19, "114" = 6), never longer rows going into the distance, which shrink to dots. Working page: `assets/styles/lantern-count.html` (Names of Allah part 1).

## The middle

- **One living object.** A single shape that is the idea (a month of days, a string of beads, a clock) stays on screen and changes with each beat.
- **One object, several roles.** The same glowing beads are the verses, then a guard ring, then a door.
- **One world, a moving anchor.** Keep one scene (a page of text, a building, a night sky) and move the focus inside it: a glow jumps to the next word, the camera finds the next lit window.
- **Several graphics, one per idea.** Each beat gets its own motion piece when the ideas differ in kind. A count, a balance, a dial, a grid: a new kind of reveal each beat, because the same reveal three times becomes predictable.
- **A new picture for each spoken line.** With a voice, every phrase gets its own scene, and the scene changes in the pause at the end of the sentence.
- **A locked stage that builds up with the voice.** The camera holds still like a theatre stage. Each scene starts empty, and each thing the voice names appears on that word (a ground line, a bed, people one by one, a light, wings), so the picture is complete when the sentence ends. Between scenes the stage clears to empty paper, a small chapter label shows alone, then the next scene builds. A big shift changes the whole room (cream to night), not the camera. A choice per reel against the travelling camera, and the two mix: travel to a place, then hold while it builds.
- **Colour as the answer.** Questions stack in plain ink, the right answer arrives under each in gold, a wrong or empty answer in grey. The verdict is read from colour before the words.
- **Split screen, or two sides of one situation.** Two lives run in sync with a shared time label. Strong when the source is about two ways of living.
- **A repeating gesture with a visible count.** The same action five times (once per prayer, with five dots filling) lets the viewer predict the next one and stay for the last.
- **A ladder of repeats.** One frame repeats with a new place each time (feet, chest, head), so the third step is expected.
- **Everyday scenes.** Simple line figures in a room: bed, window, desk. Only nameless people, never a prophet or companion (chain.md rule 7).
- **A fixed frame.** A small section label, a two-line caption with one accent word, a panel with the visual, the citation under it. The panel changes; the frame stays.
- **Named sections.** Short labels ("The turn", "The request") act as chapter markers in a long reel.
- **The text itself as the graphic.** A verse as numbered rows, a bracket splitting it, a curve linking a word to another page, bars radiating round a key word.
- **One micro-diagram per phrase.** A tiny drawing for each phrase of a du'a (a ring opening, a knot straightening), under a step tracker.
- **The key word lands just after it is said.** With a voice, the key word turns to the accent colour and an underline draws under it about 0.1 s after the voice says it.
- **A ground that changes with the story.** A sky going from night to dawn, a glow that warms as the good side adds up.
- **When a line names a force, show it acting out the line.** Lightning on "deception", water that turns into fire on "fire look like water", cracks crawling toward a shield on "trains you against", a glitch on "information warfare", fog that lifts on "clears the fog". The base look stays calm; the element comes only with its line, with a flash and a boom on the frame it lands. Ready-made: `Elements` in references/screens.md.
- **One motif that changes meaning.** One shape carries the whole reel and flips its meaning: a gold eye for truth, a red eye for the threat; the screen turns upside down for "inversion".
- **Colour that changes only when the meaning does.** Gold for protection, red for the threat, blue for water.
- **Text over a living scene (a fallback).** When a source is strong but has nothing to act out, the text tells the story and a related scene keeps the eye busy: a silhouette by a river at dusk, a palm and a stream, a storm with lightning on the hard line, rays on "He says", a night with a crescent. Each section gets its own scene that matches its line, and the scene always moves a little (water, cloud, light, particles). It is not a default, only inspiration.
- **Characters without faces, made of light.** Nameless people drawn as silhouettes with no face: men dark and rim-lit, women and children soft cream light. They act with posture and hands alone: kneel, give back a purse, reach, turn away, look up. Light says who is wronged and who holds power, so the story reads with the captions hidden. It keeps the no-faces line and still gives the viewer people to feel for. Not brush-stroke figures: the user does not want characters drawn in a few brush strokes (2026-10-26). How the figures, light and camera are built: look.md, "Cinema night".
- **Real objects with working parts.** A balance with a hook, chain, sight frame, needle, beam, cords and deep pans; coins that fly from a chest into a pan and tip it; a purse handed back; windows that light one by one for "two, three, four" and go dark to leave one. A real mechanism acting out the line beats an icon.
- **Gold only for what the story weighs.** Coins, the scale and the one thread are gold; everything else is night and cream. The eye goes where the meaning is.
- **The caption turns into the next motif.** A key line blows into sand and becomes the next thing: a caption becomes the gold thread, "Two, three, or four." becomes "Just one.", a title becomes "justice." The words and the picture are one object.
- **One question for the whole reel.** A long reel holds when one question pulls it; a list of separate facts does not.

## Moving between scenes

- **Fade, zoom or mask, rarely a hard cut.** Every strong page we studied changes scene without hard cuts: a crossfade of about 0.4 to 0.5 s, a zoom into a doorway, a curtain opening, a slide.
- **Dip to near black, rebuild from a seed.** The scene dims until one dot or a "0" is left, and the next scene grows from it.
- **The motif leads the camera.** A gold thread runs out of one scene, and a whip pan follows it into the next, with motion blur only while the camera moves. The viewer follows the thread, so the change feels like travel, not a cut.
- **Camera moves as transitions.** Crane down into a courtyard, pull back to a facade, crane up out of it to see the town, tilt up to the sky. Each move is a new place in the same world.
- **A flash that fades into the next scene.** A lightning flash on a hard line ("a great sin") fades to night, and the next scene is already there under it.
- **Colour shift instead of a cut.** The sky or ground changes mood over 2 to 3 s while the subject continues.

## The payoff

- **Let the viewer watch the work.** Something on screen makes the answer: a sweep, a pen, a fill, a chain drawing itself. Show the proof travelling into the claim.
- **Count in front of the viewer, then snap.** Numbers or marks climb (or flicker) and lock on the answer. In a race, let one side lead before both lock; answers that arrive already equal have no tension.
- **Freeze before the answer.** Everything stops for a beat, then the answer lands (the sound side is in sound.md).
- **One pulse at the payoff.** One flash or ring with one sound hit, a short hold, then move on.
- **One slam beat.** One bare word on its own screen ("Why?") breaks the rhythm once per reel.
- **Punch words last.** Build the key line word by word, with the turn at the end ("not once").
- **Hold the dark, then flood the ground.** Keep the ground dark for most of the reel and let it turn to light only at the payoff.
- **The accent colour only on the turn.** Spend it on a handful of lines in the whole reel, so each one lands.

## The ending

- **The last act jumps to the viewer's world.** Zoom out from the data to the earth, a city, a day, a phone, then give the one thing to do.
- **Fade the picture before the closing line.** The scene dims, then the closing line arrives on a quiet ground and holds (chain.md rule 4, the final read).
- **From source to action.** After the source, a checklist whose checks tick on one at a time.
- **A recap that works as a still.** A numbered recap or a full board, held about 4 s, ready to screenshot, since the caption can ask to save.
- **A closing question.** One question the viewer can answer in a comment ("Which one did you forget?").
- **A loop ending.** The last frame returns to the first image (or the opening anchor returns), with one line that turns it into an instruction.

## Pacing numbers

Measured on studied pages; starting points, not limits.

- Typed English: 22 to 28 characters per second. Arabic: word by word, about 0.25 s apart. English after Arabic: 1 to 1.5 s later.
- A reveal beat runs about 7 s: setup 0.5 s, the work 3 to 4 s, the payoff, hold 2 s, fade 1 s.
- Strong pages change something every 1.5 to 5 s and stand truly still for about a tenth of the reel; weak ones stood still for 40 to 60%.
- Shorter usually wins on the same page: the top reels ran 9 to 40 s. Never pad a reel by repeating its content.
