# Story shapes

How a reel tells its story: the order of the beats and what pulls the viewer from one to the next. video-reel-story picks one of these, mixes two, or makes a new one. None is required, and two reels in a row should not tell the same way.

## Contents

Where the story comes from · Story references · The shapes · The comment ask · Hooks · What makes a story hold

## Where the story comes from

- **A hadith or ayah.** The source is checked first (video-reel-topic). The story is built around what the source can show.
- **A YouTube video**. The video's own script is the source: keep its content and its order. Never add, drop or swap a fact without asking the user first, because the user chose that video for what it says. Lines the primary sources do not support are listed for the user's call, never fixed silently.
- **A made-up story (parable).** An ordinary, faceless person acts out one or more lessons from a checked text. `video-reel-parable` writes it and hands the reel in at `status: story done`.

## The shapes

- Hook, the idea shown working, the source, what it means, what to do.
- A question, the evidence piece by piece, the answer in the source.
- Two lives compared, then the source that explains the difference. Strongest as two figures side by side, each with a plain label over the head, given the same object or the same chance. The contrast must read with the sound off, and the last shot holds on the gap between them: one has moved on, the other still stands in the same spot.
- **Fail, try again, win.** One figure tries and fails (a thud and a small screen shake), gets up and tries again, fails again, and improves a little each time, until a success sound lands the win. Each fall must look different, so the viewer sees learning, not a loop. Fits sabr, tawbah and du'a said again and again, and the source comes after the win, never before it.
- One word of the Arabic opened up: its root, where else it appears, what it changes.
- "You already do X, but you forgot Y": a small act you can do today, one per scene, a recap, a closing question. Works as a numbered series, with the save tied to a moment ("every Thursday night").
- A count in the source: the question, the count shown working, a one-line verdict, the verse held to read.
- **The theme scan.** A feeling or topic, the search through the Qur'an shown working in our own visual (never the common bar ring), the verses found one by one with their story, a reflection clearly marked as ours, a line to the viewer. Claim "every place" only after a real search on quran.com; otherwise "some places". No unsourced science or medical lines in the reflection.
- **Read the neighbours.** A verse people quote alone: what comes just before it, the verse itself, what comes right after, then the pattern across all three ("Before it: be fair. Inside it: be fair. After it: be fair."), the condition it carries, a question to the viewer. The thread travels through one world from scene to scene.
- A very short loop (9 to 12 s): one situation, one turn, one closing line, so it replays.
- **The word-key puzzle.** A strange true fact as the hook ("a sentence said twice", "a surah that calls us ungrateful"), a promise the answer is worth staying for, a personal pull before the lesson ("think of something you're carrying"), the ayah, then one Arabic word that solves the puzzle, acted out as a picture (two bags merge into one hardship, two eases stay apart). A small count the viewer can feel (one bad day, six good), the end mirroring the start, and home to tonight with one tiny action. The user loved this way of telling.
- **A series in the video's order.** For a YouTube source in parts: the video's opening idea as the hook (adapted, not copied from another page), each item in the video's order with one citation per screen, the video's closing reflection.
- **Open loop.** Tease the answer at 0 s ("by the end you will know why"), pay it off only on the last beat. The tease must match the payoff exactly, or the viewer feels cheated.
- **Stacked loops, closed in reverse.** Open two or three questions (why this word, why this order, why this promise), then answer the last one first and the first one last. The answers cascade at the end and invite a rewatch. Three loops at most.
- **Myth, source, cost.** "Most people think X." The source shows Y. Then what believing X costs us. Draw the myth as an object the ayah breaks. Gentle, never a scare, and only a myth people really hold.
- **The twist reframe.** Build the familiar reading of a verse for about 60% of the reel, then the source (its context, its neighbours, one word) flips what it meant. Everything watched is re-read at once.
- **Countdown, best last.** "Three small things..." with a visible counter and the strongest item last. The count must be honest: three items from the sources, never a count the text does not give as if it did.
- **Zoom out.** Start on one tiny detail (a seed, a drop, a single letter), then pull the camera back step by step until the whole picture shows. Each pull back is a small surprise.
- **Before, process, after.** One heart, home or day, changed by one act, drawn step by step. A single life changing in time, where "two lives compared" is two lives side by side.
- **One day on a clock.** A clock hand travels through one day; each hour stops on one sunnah or one prayer. The moving hand shows progress and how far is left.
- **Domino chain.** One small act sets off results one after another, each tile asking "and then what?". Every link needs its own source; never invent a link to make the chain longer.
- **Imagine you.** "Imagine you have one hour left." The viewer is put inside the situation, then the source answers it. Faceless by nature: the viewer is the character.
- **Start at the end.** Open on the outcome (the full tree, the lit room), then rewind to how it began. Variant: the last shot rebuilds the first frame, changed by what was learned.
- **Answer first, then why.** Give the answer in the first 2 s, then spend the reel on "but why does it work?". For verses whose headline is easy and whose reason is not.
- **The stakes ladder.** Each beat bigger than the last (one deed, ten, seven hundred), only where the source gives the numbers. The climb pulls the viewer up.
- **Did you notice?** A verse or du'a everyone knows, read again slowly, until one detail most people miss lights up. The viewer feels they found it.
- **Pattern, then the break.** Show a repeated thing three times, hold back the fourth, and the fourth is the one the source names. Never give the break away in the hook.
- **Lesson and you, in turn.** Alternate a teaching beat (the source) with a human beat ("you at 3 am, phone in hand"). Each switch resets attention. No face needed: hands, a room, a screen.
- **The road, stop by stop.** The hook promises the whole map (how long it takes, start point, end point) and shows a count of what is coming. Then one road, one direction, each stop with the same parts (a plain title, the thing acted out, one source), a tracker so the viewer knows what is left, small turns inside stops, and the opening image returning with a new meaning at the end. Fits anything with stops, steps, parts or a sequence: a story, a ritual, a life, an argument.
- **Cliffhanger series.** Part 1 ends on an open question that Part 2 answers ("Part 2 tomorrow"). Each part must still pay off something of its own. The hook of a later part works alone; the link to the part before comes right after it, and the tease for the next part comes before the comment ask (rules.md, Copy).

## The comment ask

Every reel ends by asking for one word in the comments, on the last beat and in the caption. The dhikr reel's "comment done" drove its reach. Pick the word the viewer would say anyway at the end, so it is easy and it means something:

- Did something along with the reel (dhikr, a du'a): "done".
- Saw Allah's power or a wonder (Buraq, Jibril, 600 wings): "SubhanAllah".
- Heard a du'a or a hope: "Ameen".
- Felt grateful or relieved: "Alhamdulillah".
- Learned a word or a fact: the word itself ("sakinah").

Change the word from reel to reel; the same word every time reads as a habit, not a feeling.

## Hooks

The first line, said or written, in the first 2 s. Visual and sound openings are in [ideas.md](ideas.md), "The opening". Every hook must be true to the source word for word; the hook is the highest-risk line (rules.md, Source). Never a riddle hook, a scare, a guilt hook, clickbait the source cannot back, or another page's hook line.

The formulas live in [hooks.json](hooks.json): 23 shapes, each with a template, an example, the on-screen line, what it is for and its **trap** (how it gets ruined). Its examples show the shape only; they are not checked sources. The formulas are a toolbox, never a limit: a hook in a new shape is fine when it passes the same rules.

Per reel (video-reel-story step 3): write **three hooks from three different formulas**, each as a spoken line and an on-screen line (six words or fewer). Score the spoken lines:

```bash
python3 <video-reel>/scripts/hookscore.py hooks.txt
```

- **OUT** (a guilt, scare, riddle or preamble line) is dropped, whatever it scores.
- **Under 50** means the hook is not there yet. Rewrite before the copy goes on.
- **CHECK SOURCE** names an absolute word ("only", "never", "every"). Confirm it on the primary page.
- The score is a floor, not the judge. Pick the hook that is truest to the source and best fits the story's first picture; say why in one line.

## What makes a story hold

- **One question pulls the whole reel.** A long reel holds when one question pulls it; a list of separate facts does not.
- **Pick a story with a lot for the motion to act out** (weather, seasons, travel, a change), never a riddle hook ("Who held the pencil?") or a still object with marks.
- **The source must be showable**: an object, a place, an action, a change or a count. A pure dialogue goes to a carousel, or gets an outside analogy marked as ours (rules.md, Source).
- **Plan the reveal order.** Decide what the viewer learns first and what is held back for later; a story told in plain time order is only one choice. Start at the end, in the middle, or with a question, when the material calls for it.
- **Every part earns its place.** Something moves the reel on every 5 to 8 s (new information, a turn, an open question, a visual reveal), but the big moments get room to breathe; never rush the beat the reel is for. (Creator guides give 4 to 12 s; none is platform data.)
- **Three open loops at most,** and open a new one as one closes.
- **The payoff matches the tease.** A loud start with a small answer loses trust for the next reel.
- **The first frame reads with the sound off,** because many watch muted.
- Openings, payoffs and endings that work: [ideas.md](ideas.md).
