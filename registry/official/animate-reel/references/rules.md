# Reel rules checklist

Every rule a reel must pass, in one place, so none is missed. Copy this file into the reel folder as `rules.md` at the start (animate-reel-topic). Tick each box as its step finishes and write a few words of evidence after it. A rule that does not apply gets `N/A` and the reason. Do not deliver while a box is empty.

**Keep it complete.** When the user sets a new rule or corrects a reel, add the rule here in the same turn, under the step it belongs to, with its reason. Remove a rule only when the user drops it. The rules are about getting a reel right; none of them limits the direction, look or motion.

## Questions (the /animate-reel gate)

- [ ] One question round asked what was not already given: source, mood (soft or intense; it never narrows the looks pitched), look, length, voiceover (yes or no; never a default). Each question had options and a recommended pick.

## Source (animate-reel-topic)

Islamic pages (`niche: islamic` in `00 - notes/context/declared/brand.md`) tick every box below. Every other niche: the boxes about Arabic, hadith, tafsir, Qur'an stories and the speaker get `N/A (niche: <niche>)`, and this box is ticked instead:

- [ ] Every claim has a primary link (an official site, a study, a book page) read with web fetch, recorded under `## Sources` with its title, URL and the quoted line. A search summary, a repost or memory is a lead, never the source.

- [ ] Arabic, English and number copied from the primary page (sunnah.com or quran.com), not from search or memory.
- [ ] Hadith: sahih or hasan only (a recognised grader's *jayyid* or *sahih isnad* counts), Bukhari and Muslim first, grading recorded. Never da'if or fabricated. A companion's words are never shown as the Prophet's ﷺ.
- [ ] Classical tafsir is a source too (Ibn Kathir, al-Tabari, Ibn Abbas through them), read in the book itself, not a summary. A tafsir detail is told as the tafsir's, never as the Qur'an's words, and any hadith it carries is graded like any other.
- [ ] Every number, count and absolute ("only", "never", "once") checked against the primary text, method recorded. The hook is the highest-risk line.
- [ ] Pattern and count reels (word frequencies, structure, symmetry): the counting method (which text, what counts as one) is shown on screen, and each claim is labelled verified, a scholar's view, a coincidence or disputed. Never make a coincidence up. A science claim is rare and always shows its label on screen.
- [ ] The source itself has something showable: an object, a place, an action, a change or a count that motion can act out. A pure dialogue or list (Muslim 395, Allah's replies to Al-Fatiha) has nothing to show, so any picture is decoration over text; send it to a carousel, or tell it as text over a living, story-matched scene (ideas.md, "Text over a living scene"). No metaphors: many viewers do not get them and they annoy people. A plain real-life example is fine when it makes the idea clearer, marked as ours ("think of it like...") and never stated as something the source says.
- [ ] Ideas start from Qur'an stories (qasas) first: they have places, events and twists that motion can act out. Good ways in: a why-question people already ask, a paradox in the story, a feeling or struggle the story answers, or a plain real-life example.
- [ ] For a YouTube source, the video's script is trusted: its story, its images (the 99 doors) and its claims stay in. A number or claim the video states is checked (count it, read it), never dropped just because it is unchecked; only a claim the primary text contradicts goes to the user.
- [ ] Who speaks in the quoted text is checked ("they said" versus "Allah said"), because a misattributed speaker changes the meaning.
- [ ] No other page's signature move copied (its look, its catchphrase, its hook line): borrow principles only.
- [ ] A made-up story (a parable, `animate-reel-parable`) is allowed: the person never lived, but every lesson rests on a checked text, or is plain basic deen. It is never shown as a real event or a hadith, and it claims nothing the text does not say.
- [ ] A true story (Qur'an, hadith, seerah) gets no made-up dialogue, inner thoughts or details, and keeps the order of events the source gives. Drama comes from what is revealed when, never from changing what happened. Only a parable invents.

## Direction (animate-reel-story and animate-reel-style)

- [ ] Two or three directions that differ in kind, and the user picked one.
- [ ] Each direction names its look (a recipe, a mix or something new) and its colours. The user chooses the look; a silent default pushes every reel toward the last one.
- [ ] Nothing pitched smaller because a kit makes it easier. Kits and styles are a toolbox, never a limit.
- [ ] Looks are pitched as clear, different options across the range, light and dark (cinematic and the Cinema night look included), never narrowed by my own reading of the mood or story. The user picks.
- [ ] A style is a look (material, palette, texture, light, motion), never its props or topics. Any object and any topic can use any style; a recipe's props and "good for" list are examples only (Clay POV does not need its phone). Build the objects the script needs in the style.
- [ ] No prophet drawn, in any form. Companions only as faceless figures.
- [ ] No AI-generated video ever. No AI images unless the user agreed an exception for this one reel (then backgrounds only: no creatures, people or the Ka'bah). All motion is drawn in code.
- [ ] Motion graphics, not a text carousel: every line has its own visual event acting it out, with proper illustration, planned line by line in animate-reel-story and the beat table, not beat by beat. A line shares the scene before it only when it is under about 1.5 s or only adds the citation, and the plan says why. Looks pitched side by side each have their own story device and motion system.
- [ ] Every visual carries the message without a caption. Test each line with its caption hidden: the scene must still say it. Act out the words ("however hard you try" is three real tries). Pictures may add details the words do not say (what a character hides, what the viewer knows and he does not). The words still say everything plainly; the picture only adds.
- [ ] Every figure acts out the story; none stands in a scene for mood alone (a lone man walking a desert at sunset). Particles, light and glow do something in the story, never background glitter. Worn-out pictures (chains for sin, a clock running out, light at the end of a road, a generic mosque) need a twist that belongs to this story, because the viewer has seen them a hundred times.
- [ ] One motif transforms through the reel and the camera travels place to place inside one world (Roots: seeds to garden; Halves: lantern halves to a street of lights). The user loved this most.
- [ ] The shapes carry the meaning: things that "never click" never overlap or close the gap, and their edges clearly differ.
- [ ] When a line names a force (lightning, fire, water, fog, cracks, light), it is shown acting out the line on the frame it lands.

## Copy (animate-reel-story) and storyboard (animate-reel-storyboard)

- [ ] A story told to one person, in full warm sentences, in the voice set in `00 - notes/context/declared/brand.md` (until `00 - notes/context/observed/taste.md` has lessons, a plain warm neutral voice). The shape is new for every reel; blunt fragments read as cold. Pick a story with a lot for the motion to act out (weather, seasons, travel, a change), never a riddle hook ("Who held the pencil?") or a still object with marks, and plan camera moments per beat (push in, whip pan, crane up, a 180 turn) in the beat table. 2D reels get camera moves too: push-ins, pans, tilts, parallax layers that slide at different speeds, and rack focus; a flat locked frame is not enough.
- [ ] The reel ends with a comment ask: one word or short phrase that fits this reel ("SubhanAllah", "Ameen", "done"), on the last beat and in the caption. It comes from what the viewer feels or did at the end, never the same word on every reel. The dhikr reel's "comment done" drove its reach. No other send line unless earned and agreed.
- [ ] A reel in parts: the hook works alone for a stranger who never saw the part before, because most viewers arrive from Explore. The link to the part before comes right after the hook, in one short line ("In Part 1, we met..."). A small "Part N" tag sits at the top and on the cover. Every part but the last ends with one tease line for the next part, before the comment ask.
- [ ] Nothing basic or overused. The test is the angle, not the topic: would the viewer think "this again"? A missed fajr told a new way passes; another "a kindness that travels" does not. Lessons heard a thousand times (always be kind, never give up, be grateful, don't judge, charity after death) need an angle that makes the viewer rethink something.
- [ ] Story check run before the copy is shown (animate-reel-story step 4), clarity first: a basic Muslim (who knows who Musa is, what salah is) understands every line on one listen.
- [ ] No em dashes. No jargon.
- [ ] Islamic pages: scholars and tafsir may be named in the copy and on screen ("Ibn Kathir explains...") when the work was actually read. Every other niche: experts and authors may be named when their work was actually read. Never a vague "scholars say" or "experts say".
- [ ] No guilt or shame hooks ("You didn't get up"). A hook can name a real struggle gently, never sting the viewer.
- [ ] Three hooks from three formulas in hooks.json, scored with hookscore.py; none OUT, the pick at 50 or more, all three and the pick under `## Hooks` in script.md.
- [ ] The beat table names, for every line, its composition (what is in front, what is behind, what changes) and why the shot is there. Shot sizes vary on purpose: wide places, close objects, abstract shapes, type-led screens. "Show a wall" is not direction; say what makes this wall matter.
- [ ] The beat table names, for every beat, the transition into it: continuous (what carries over and how it moves) or a scene change in the medium's own way, and why. Not a cut on every beat, not one forced shot.
- [ ] One hero sequence: named in the treatment, choreographed move by move under the beat table (anticipation, main move, overshoot, follow-through, settle, secondary motion, camera), and drawn as the storyboard's hero frame. It goes beyond illustrating the words.
- [ ] `storyboard.html` approved by the user before the build: quick 2D sketch panels for every beat (times, exact text, camera arrow, palette swatches, transition, sounds, assumed facts) plus one hero frame in the real style code, so the story and the look are agreed before the full world is built.

- [ ] Recitation: the Qur'an is never read by a computer voice. A clip of a human reciter is allowed, with Sheikh Sa'ud ash-Shuraim first (quran.com qdc reciter 10), then Yasser ad-Dossary (97). Cut it from the full-surah file with the ayah timings from `https://api.qurancdn.com/api/qdc/audio/reciters/<id>/audio_files?chapter=<n>&segments=true`; the reciter's name goes on screen while it plays; any voiceover and sound bed stop under it.

*Preferred, not a box:* narrate the story (what happened, how one event led to the next) and put the reference under it, like `QURAN 3:47`. Reading or quoting the translation is fine whenever it fits the reel.

## Build and look (animate-reel-build)

- [ ] Colours come from the agreed look and palette, and the end beat uses the same palette. The page's own colours (`colours` in `00 - notes/context/declared/brand.md`) are never a silent default, and "pastel" means exploring many pastel palettes, not one stock set.
- [ ] Citation line under the source text, one per screen. Islamic pages: `SAHIH AL-BUKHARI 6464` or `QURAN 18:19`. Every other niche: the source name in spaced capitals, such as `WORLD HEALTH ORGANIZATION`.
- [ ] Every Qur'an Arabic on screen has its English translation right under it, copied word for word from quran.com (The Clear Quran), at least 40 px. Our own captions may paraphrase; the translation line never does.
- [ ] Phone sizes on 1080x1920: headlines at least 64 px, labels the story needs at least 40 px, key numbers at least 96 px, Arabic at least 90 px. Text between y 250 and y 1500. Each beat fills most of the frame.
- [ ] The style's texture and finishing passes (grain, pooled ink, glow) run on every frame at the recipe's strength; thinning them makes the medium read as fake.
- [ ] `frame(t)` is pure: all randomness seeded, so the same t always gives the same frame and the render matches the preview.
- [ ] Arabic in Scheherazade New with full tashkeel, shaped as a whole string, never Amiri (it drops dots in Chrome). Key Arabic (up to 10 words) written with the pen.
- [ ] Every Arabic dot and mark checked on a full-size crop, by pixel count or at 2x zoom. The dots are about 11 px, and both an agent and the main thread missed real dots by eye on shrunk stills.
- [ ] Pen reveal: one pass per line, the reveal band tall enough for every letter and mark at every frame, the vowel marks on their own layer that comes in last, and the unvowelled layer cross-faded out so no ghost double of a letter stays. Stills every 0.4 s through the writing.
- [ ] No six-pointed star (hexagram) or other Israeli symbol anywhere, and no logo, stamp, seal, badge or emblem, including an 8-point star in a box. Loose 5-point stars and open 8-, 10- or 12-point Islamic geometry are fine. Check every star, tile, sparkle and bokeh shape.
- [ ] Captions sit on a calm part of the picture with a soft dark backing glow: never over busy detail, never gold text on a bright gold area.
- [ ] The live preview was shown to the user before any render, because a render made without one had to be redone.
- [ ] Helper-built pages: the helper takes one sheet (one still per beat), fixes only what is clearly broken and hands over, with no scoring or re-capture rounds. The main agent does the one full still check itself in step 6; a helper's "checked" is not proof, and two full checks waste time and usage.
- [ ] Pace: about 0.2 s a word plus 0.7 s per line once it lands (0.3 s a word felt very slow), something new every 1.5 to 5 s, the final read holds 2 to 5 s.
- [ ] End beat: the comment ask, then the page handle (from `00 - notes/context/declared/brand.md`) near y 1420, then "Follow for more."
- [ ] Light watermark: the handle small near y 1475 (about 30px, opacity about .2) on every frame, fading out over about .5 s as the end card shows the full handle; it never overlaps on-screen text.
- [ ] 1080x1920, 30 fps.
- [ ] Stills at the start, middle and end of every beat, each one looked at full size.
- [ ] Motion clips (`capture.mjs clip`) of the hero sequence and every scene change, each strip looked at: a good still can hide bad motion.
- [ ] No fix changes something the user already approved (storyboard or live preview) without asking first, in one line: what changes and why.
- [ ] The scores come from a cold reader, a fresh helper that sees only the pack (`references/review.md`, part B), never from the maker, because a maker scores about 2 points high (take-one, 2026-11-03).
- [ ] Every fix after the user or a reader has seen the reel has a fix card in `script.md` under `## Fixes`, and passes the scope check: `capture.mjs scope` shows every frame outside the fix identical (video-director, 2026-11-03, so a fix never changes what nobody asked about).
- [ ] Stills and clips scored 1 to 10 through four lenses (story, art direction, motion, phone viewer), scores and the worst 3 problems written in `script.md`, those fixed; stop at all 8 or more, or after 2 fix rounds, because each round costs usage.
- [ ] Easing and timing follow the motion numbers in look.md (or the recipe's own): nothing the eye follows moves linearly, nothing scales up from zero, one hero move at a time, key poses pushed for a phone, things with weight on springs (`assets/motion.js`).
- [ ] Text contrast at least 4.5:1 on every frame; at most 3 bright flashes in any second.
- [ ] Camera and timing follow look.md, "Camera and timing": clear shot sizes (wide, medium close-up, close-up) chosen for the line, never two of the same size in a row, the world edge never shown, and text that appears only after the camera lands, on a layer outside the camera.
- [ ] Entrances take 0.7 to 1.3 s and exits take 0.35 to 0.55 s. Every scene has 4 to 6 small living details, each a pure function of `t`.
- [ ] The area right behind every caption reads dark (25% brightness or less) or light (80% or more) on a grayscale still, never mid-grey.
- [ ] Reading check passed: the page exposes `window.TEXTS(t)` (contract at the top of `scripts/readcheck.mjs`) and `node <skill>/scripts/readcheck.mjs <folder>` prints no FAIL: every text held at least 0.2 s a word plus 0.7 s, fully in the frame.
- [ ] Flicker test passed once per reel: `node <skill>/scripts/capture.mjs <folder> verify` (12 frames drawn twice must match).
- [ ] Stills checked on the sheet with the shaded Instagram zones (`sheet.mjs` shades above y 250 and below y 1500): no text in red.

## Sound (animate-reel-storyboard plans it, animate-reel-build mixes it)

- [ ] Inside the user's sound line: hits, booms, risers, pings, beats, loops, nature beds and foley are fine; no haram music. Ask only if a sound may carry a melody. No voice unless asked.
- [ ] Every sound starts on the exact frame its event appears.
- [ ] Sparse: only the big moments get a sound, about one every 3 to 5 s (a 50 s reel has roughly 10 to 15), because a busy mix is hard to listen to (a viewer said so about the Roots reel, which had 46). Small moves stay silent.
- [ ] One sound at a time, with quiet gaps between; no stacked pairs (a pop plus a ding on the same moment is one too many).
- [ ] No scratchy sounds: pen, pencil, chalk or marker writing; paper, fabric or plastic slides. Card slides (`slide`) are fine. Other sounds are picked per reel; the fixed level in mix.py keeps them from turning sharp.
- [ ] Typing considered for text that types on: soft, quiet key presses, as in the walyatalattaf reel; one typed line counts as one sound.
- [ ] Drawing is silent, or one soft whoosh as a line starts; no pen scratch. Counters tick only on the last few steps.
- [ ] No constant hum or drone anywhere in the mix: the user finds it irritating.
- [ ] Sound weight matches the reel: a soft or warm reel has only light sounds (glass, plink, drop, bell, tick, wood, tap, key) and no `beat`, `thud`, `bong`, `boom` or `rise`.
- [ ] Intense reels only: middle reveals get a soft `beat`; the full hit (`rise` + `boom`) only once, on the ending beat, because on repeat it is sharp to the ears.
- [ ] Nature beds and foley that fit the scene were considered and used where they fit.
- [ ] Nature bed felt more than heard: below -36 LUFS integrated, measured on its own. Test: if you notice the bed on the first listen, it is too loud; you should only miss it when it is muted. mix.py prints `bed alone` and `BED TOO LOUD` above -36; the gain differs per bed (birds .38 gave -30.8, so about .19 for -36).
- [ ] With a voice: `python3 -I <skill>/scripts/voice_check.py <folder>` prints no FAIL (every line heard back as written, no tag read aloud). Lines marked LISTEN hold Arabic, which Whisper cannot check: name them to the user to listen to.
- [ ] A near-silence before the turn, and the sound carries at least one scene change (sound.md, Timing and layering).
- [ ] No moment above -18 LUFS momentary, voice and sounds together (the user: "18 is the max"). `mix.py` names each sound that goes over; with a voice, the sounds under it must be lowered until none is named.
- [ ] The bed stays passive, behind the content: mix.py makes it far away (dull above 1.2 kHz, a soft room), steady and low, and dips it under each effect and about 10 dB under the voice while it talks; if it still reads as part of the content, lower its gain in bed.txt.
- [ ] Normal level: `mix.py` gives every reel the same fixed boost and no loudness target, so a sparse reel is quieter overall, not louder per sound; peak at or under -1 dB. Set each sound's volume in the page.
- [ ] Kit sounds are placed on their moment: `add(t, 'kit:<id>', v)` takes the moment the sound meets (a whoosh: the middle of its move), and `mix.py` starts the file `sync.at` early. Never subtract `sync.at` in the page, because the sound then plays early twice over.

## Deliver (animate-reel-build)

- [ ] `scripts/pacing_check.py` run on the final MP4: nothing flashing under 0.3 s, or each flagged spot looked at and explained (a planned lightning flicker is fine). Hold lengths are not checked by script: the user judges them in the preview.
- [ ] The fast-action strips it writes (`reel-strip-<s>.jpg`, 12 frames in a row at the two fastest moments and at every flash) looked at for pops and jumps a contact sheet misses.
- [ ] Its sync report read: every WARN looked at, and a sound that plays before its picture moved to the frame the change starts or stops.
- [ ] Contact sheet of the final MP4 looked at.
- [ ] Packaging in `script.md`: cover text and one short caption carrying the comment ask; a parable's caption also says "An original parable."
- [ ] The Instagram and YouTube caption opens with the comment ask as its very first line, said once only, because Instagram hides everything after the first line behind "more". Threads shows the whole text, so its caption may keep the ask lower.
- [ ] `scripts/caption_check.py` run on each caption (Instagram, YouTube, Threads): no FAIL, every WARN decided out loud.
- [ ] The user told the path, the direction in one line, and what was not checked (nobody listens to the sound but the user).

## After the user posts

- [ ] Ledger row in `00 - notes/ledger.md`; post links asked for.
- [ ] Reel video posted on Threads in the same turn (the video itself, with a short caption; never a text chain).
- [ ] Idea note set to `status: posted`; dashboard refreshed.
- [ ] Instagram hashtags: the hashtags in `00 - notes/context/declared/brand.md`.
- [ ] Stats at 48 h: once the post is 48 hours old, pull it from Zernio (`analytics_get_analytics`, the Instagram account) and add a row to `## Stats at 48 h` in `00 - notes/ledger.md`: id, views, skip rate, average watch against length, shares, saves, comments.
