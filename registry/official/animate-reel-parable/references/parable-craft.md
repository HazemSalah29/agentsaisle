# Parable craft

What made the studied parable reels work, and sparks to start from. Sparks only: invent past them every time.

## Contents

## What works

Learned from studied parable reels that beat their own account by a wide margin.

- **Start from a pain or a wish the viewer has this week:** envy of someone's life, a door that closed, too many problems at once, anger at someone you love, feeling too far from Allah, the same sin again. Never from a value or a topic.
- **The text answers that pain with comfort or a tool** (an ayah that changes how it looks, a du'a to say), so the viewer saves it or sends it. Manners toward other people (greet the driver, check news, keep a secret) is advice nobody needs tonight.
- **The lesson can be famous.** "Look at those below you" got 862k. The viewer recognises the text and feels it in a new picture.
- **One faceless figure who is anyone.** No name, no job, no town. Posture carries the feeling. A short film about a stranger (a barber, a night nurse) is watched, not lived.
- **One plain picture with labels in the viewer's words:** a door that says DREAM JOB, a weight that says DEBT, "I WISH I HAD A CAR, AT LEAST." The label is what makes it plain. An unlabelled symbol the viewer must decode (a snow globe, a paper boat, a ball of clay) is a metaphor that fails.
- **It builds by repeating three times or more,** often with a counter (×1, ×2, ×3).
- **One reveal shows what he could not see:** zoom out to the end of the line, turn round to what opened behind him.
- **Few words:** about 30 words of story, every line 8 words or fewer. "He thought... He never saw..." then the turn.
- **The text is the punchline,** Arabic, English, one citation, then a two-word action that becomes the comment ask.
- **Length follows the story; aim for 60 to 110 s.** A short cap makes a parable thin. The story fills the time, never a lecture after it: more scenes, more repeats, a bigger build before the turn, and the text landing inside the story at the turn. Sabr Stories' 61 to 69 s lecture reels got 651 and 4k; in "Ungrateful" (157 s) the horse story runs to the end. The hook leaves a question open until near the end ("Stay with me"). Long with nothing left to find out still fails. Readable with the sound off; a short clip of a known scholar's voice can carry it.
- **The ending gives the viewer one of four things:** a tool to do now, a feeling he already has explained, comfort for a pain, or an answer to a question he already hears. A moral about someone else gives none of these.
- **A number that climbs on screen** (steps away, beads, days, attempts) holds the eye with the sound off. Almost every hit of the week had one.

## The basic test

The lesson being well known is not the problem. What fails is:

- a story about someone else, not the viewer;
- a plot that needs years or many people;
- a symbol with no label;
- manners advice instead of an answer to a pain;
- a soft ending with no reveal;
- a worn picture (the seed with roots underneath);
- the same formula used again within a week.

Before keeping an idea, answer all five:

1. The viewer's pain or wish in six words, in their words: would one in three feel it this month?
2. Can the first frame show it with a label, with no name and no setting?
3. What repeats or counts up three times?
4. What did he not see?
5. Would the viewer save it or send it?

Drop it if 1 or 5 fails.

**Never take an idea another page already made.** Before showing an idea, search the reels the user shared and the `proof:` lines of every idea note for the same text. If another page already told it, drop it: the user wants ideas nobody has posted.

Before showing any idea, check it against every note in `00 - notes/ideas/` with `status: dropped`. If an idea shares its text (the same hadith or ayah number) or its lesson with a dropped note, do not show it. When the user rejects a new idea, set its note to `status: dropped` in the same turn.

## Sparks

Ways a parable can be built, to start thinking. None is required, and the best idea may fit none of them.

Things people do that rarely get told:

- Envy that slowly eats the one who feels it.
- Becoming the person you once hated.
- Being feared mistaken for being respected.
- Judging others while hiding the same sin.
- How power changes the way someone treats the people under him.
- Giving up everything for something that turns out to mean nothing.
- Talking yourself into anything.
- Remembering Allah only when the life you built falls apart.
- The gap between how someone looks in public and what he does alone.
- "I will repent later", and the time that was never promised.

Ways to tell it:

- The viewer knows something the character does not.
- Two stories that seem unrelated and meet at the end.
- A choice whose cost shows only at the end.
- A character who gets exactly what he wanted.
- A choice between two things he truly loves.
- One small act that shows who someone really is.
- An object that changes meaning as the story goes.
- The same day replayed with one change.
- Several lessons in one story, each one a stop on the way.

## Checks before a parable is done

- Passes the basic test and all five hit questions.
- Every lesson traced to a checked text that truly fits, or plainly basic deen.
- No line the text does not support; an outside picture is marked as ours where the copy explains it.
- Nothing shown as a real event or as a hadith; no words given to the Prophet ﷺ; the caption says "An original parable."
- No prophet or companion drawn or named; no guilt aimed at the viewer.
- The picture idea still tells the story with every caption hidden.
- The comment ask fits the ending.
